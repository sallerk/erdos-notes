# Erdos problem #548 (Erdos-Sos conjecture) - counterexample search

**No counterexample was found.  This does NOT prove the conjecture holds at
n = 15.**  A heuristic search that finds nothing has ruled out only the graphs
it looked at, which is a vanishing fraction of the space.  See "What this does
not say" at the end.

In one paragraph: the target instance named in my instructions was wrong on
both counts, so I re-derived it - the first open case at n = 15 is **trees on
10 vertices at 61 edges**, not trees on 11 vertices at 46 edges (the brief's
instance is *closed* by a 2014 theorem).  I built an exact tree-containment
test, checked it against brute force and against a second independent
algorithm, and ran about **1.4 billion** simulated-annealing graph evaluations
across seven instances, one dedicated search per tree.  Nothing came close: at
the primary instance the scarcest tree that could legally be the missing one
still had roughly 4 x 10^7 distinct embeddings, where a counterexample needs 0.
Every graph reported here is saved as JSON and re-checked by a verifier that
shares no code with the search.

---

## 1. What the conjecture says, and what the right target actually is

Erdos-Sos: if a graph G on n vertices has average degree greater than t-2,
then G contains **every** tree on t vertices.  Equivalently, if

    e(G) > n(t-2)/2 ,   i.e.   e(G) >= floor(n(t-2)/2) + 1 ,

then every tree of order t is a subgraph of G.  A counterexample is one graph
plus one tree it fails to contain.

**The target instance in my instructions was wrong, in two independent ways.**
Details and quotes are in `LITERATURE.md`; the short version:

* The brief said "n = 15, at least 46 edges, some tree on 11 vertices".
  The edge arithmetic is wrong: for a tree on 11 vertices at n = 15 the
  threshold is 68, not 46.  (46 is what the formula gives for a tree on
  8 vertices.)  **[VERIFIED]**
* More importantly, the brief inherited a convention slip from the comment
  thread on erdosproblems.com.  The site writes "tree on k+1 vertices"; every
  source paper writes "tree of order k", and the comment copies the papers'
  `k` without translating.  You can see the slip inside the comment itself: it
  says Zhou proved the case "graph G has k vertices", which under the site's
  own convention would mean a graph too small to hold the tree.
  Re-indexed by tree order t, the closed cases are: t <= 9 (Eaton-Tiner,
  Tiner-Tomlin) for every n, and n <= t+4 (Zhou, Slater-Teo-Yap, Wozniak,
  Tiner, Yuan-Zhang) for every t.  **[VERIFIED against Yuan-Zhang Thm 1.8 and
  Tiner's thesis abstract]**

At n = 15 that leaves **exactly one open tree order**: t = 10.  (t <= 9 is
closed outright; t >= 11 is closed because then n <= t+4.)  In particular the
brief's own target, trees on 11 vertices at n = 15, is *closed* by Yuan-Zhang,
since 15 = 11+4.

> **Corrected first open instance: n = 15 vertices, at least 61 edges, some
> tree on 10 vertices.**  Threshold = floor(15*8/2)+1 = **61**.  **[VERIFIED]**

### The threshold 61 is exactly best possible

An 8-regular graph on 15 vertices has 8*15/2 = 60 edges and maximum degree 8,
so it does not contain the star on 10 vertices, K_{1,9}.  Such graphs exist -
the circulant C_15(1,2,3,4) is one.  So 60 edges is not enough and 61 is the
right threshold: a counterexample would have to sit exactly **one edge above a
tight extremal example**.  Saved as `extremal_n15_t10_e60.json`; my code
confirms it misses exactly one of the 106 trees (the star) and contains the
other 105.  **[VERIFIED by two independent algorithms]**

### Why fixing the edge count at exactly 61 loses nothing

If some graph with more than 61 edges missed a tree T, deleting edges down to
61 still misses T (deleting edges never creates a subgraph copy) and still
meets the threshold.  So searching only 61-edge graphs is without loss of
generality.  **[VERIFIED - one-line argument]**

## 3. Machinery, and how it was checked

* **Tree catalogue.**  All free trees on 9, 10, 11 vertices, generated by leaf
  augmentation with an AHU canonical form, then certified two ways: counts
  match OEIS A000055 (47 / 106 / 235, and 551 at 12 vertices), and
  `sum over classes of t!/|Aut(T)| = t^(t-2)`, Cayley's count of labelled
  trees, with |Aut| computed by definition-level backtracking.  That equality
  certifies simultaneously that the catalogue is complete and that no two
  entries are isomorphic.  **[VERIFIED]**
* **Containment test.**  Exact backtracking for an injective homomorphism, with
  degree and free-neighbour pruning; it reports "found", "exhausted" (a proof
  of absence), or "hit the node cap".  All bitmask integer arithmetic.
* **Verification of the verifier.**  `test_core.py` checks it against
  brute force over all injective maps (400 random small cases, 0 mismatches),
  against an independent inclusion-exclusion algorithm (120 cases, 0
  mismatches), against exact embedding *counts* from that same independent
  algorithm (60 cases, 0 mismatches), and against hand-known facts: every tree
  on 11 vertices embeds in K_15 and in K_11; no tree on 10 vertices embeds in a
  perfect matching; K_9 u K_6 (51 edges) misses all 106 trees of order 10 but
  contains all 47 of order 9; the 8-regular circulant misses exactly the star.
  **[VERIFIED]**
* **The independent verifier** (`verify_containment.py`) shares no code with
  the search.  It decides containment by Mobius inversion,
  `inj(T,G) = sum_U (-1)^(t-|U|) C(n-|U|, t-|U|) hom(T, G[U])`, evaluated in
  uint64 (exact mod 2^64; the true value is far below 2^64).

## 4. Structural narrowing before searching

`structure.py` (output in `structure_out.txt`) derives, for the target
instance:

| fact | status |
|---|---|
| every 61-edge graph on 15 vertices has a component on >= 10 vertices (best all-small split is 9+6 = 51 edges) | VERIFIED |
| if the largest component has 11 vertices the graph is exactly K_11 u K_4, which contains everything; so a counterexample has a component on >= 12 vertices | VERIFIED |
| Delta(G) >= 9, so the star K_{1,9} IS contained and is not the missing tree | VERIFIED |
| delta(G) <= 5, since Eaton-Tiner proves delta >= t-4 = 6 forces every tree of order 10 | CONDITIONAL on Eaton-Tiner Thm 1.4 |
| delta(G) >= 5: if some vertex had degree <= 4, deleting it leaves 14 vertices and >= 57 edges, which is the threshold at (n=14, t=10), and 14 = t+4, so Yuan-Zhang's theorem applies and G-v already contains every tree of order 10 | CONDITIONAL on Yuan-Zhang Thm 1.8 |
| **therefore delta(G) = 5 exactly**, and deleting a degree-5 vertex leaves a 14-vertex 56-edge graph that must miss the same tree | CONDITIONAL |
| the missing tree is not a spider (Fan-Hong-Liu), has diameter >= 5 (Mclennan), and has no vertex with >= 3 leaf-neighbours (Sidorenko, Eaton-Tiner): **42 of the 106 trees survive** | CITED |

The delta = 5 deduction is the useful one: it rules out the obvious candidate
family "8-regular graph plus one edge" (those have delta = 8), and the
unconstrained search does in fact drift straight into that provably-safe
region, so the constrained runs are the ones that matter.

## 5. The search

### 5.1 Method

For each instance (graph order `n`, tree order `t`) the edge count is fixed at
the threshold and simulated annealing walks the space of graphs with exactly
that many edges; a move swaps one edge for one non-edge, so the edge count
never changes.  One annealing job **targets a single tree**: since a
counterexample only needs *one* missing tree, running one dedicated search per
tree is both the right decomposition and about a hundred times faster per step
than scoring the whole catalogue.  The objective is

    1e15 * (target tree PROVED absent)          <- the jackpot
  + 1e11 * (undecided at the node cap)
  + 1e6  * (60 - log2(1 + hom(T,G)))            <- smooth, exact, deterministic
  + 1e2  * (backtracking nodes to find one embedding)
  + a flat bonus for delta(G) <= t-5            <- the Eaton-Tiner region

`hom(T,G)`, the number of tree homomorphisms, is an exact deterministic tree
DP.  An earlier version used a randomised (Knuth) estimator of the embedding
count; the annealer optimised the estimator's *noise* rather than the graph, so
it was replaced.  The verdict itself is never the objective: it comes from the
exact backtracking test, and across all 1.1 billion evaluations below the
containment test was never left undecided at its node cap (`undec` column).

Sanity check on the optimiser: told to target the star K_{1,9} at (15,10,61),
six independent runs all reached exactly 725760 = 2*9! embeddings with degree
sequence 8^13 9^2 - which is provably the minimum possible at 61 edges.

any single tree achieved by any graph the search visited - the closest miss;
0 would be a counterexample.  "min over viable trees" restricts to the trees
that the proved tree-side theorems have not already excluded, which is the
number that actually matters.

### 5.4b The sub-instance forced by delta = 5 (run G)

If a counterexample exists at (15, 10, 61) then, by the delta = 5 argument,
deleting a degree-5 vertex leaves a graph on 14 vertices with exactly 56 edges
that misses the *same* tree - and that tree cannot be the star, because 61
edges force a vertex of degree 9.  Run G searched that instance directly:
318,000,000 evaluations, one dedicated run per tree.

The only tree the search was ever able to drive to zero embeddings there is
tree 54, **the star** - realised by 8-regular graphs on 14 vertices, which are
exactly the extremal examples at 56 edges and are excluded by the argument
above.  The scarcest non-star tree reached 1.7e6 embeddings; the scarcest
*viable* tree reached 2.2e7.  So the necessary condition was searched hard and
never met.  **[ASSERTED - heuristic search, not a proof]**

### 5.4c Bugs found and fixed during the work (recorded, not hidden)

1. The first annealer silently **destroyed edges**: a min-degree loop reused
   the loop variable `v`, which clobbered the variable holding the move being
   undone, so rejected moves were not restored.  It produced "graphs missing
   106 trees" that had 14 edges instead of 61.  Caught by adding an explicit
   edge-count invariant to every returned graph, which is now checked on every
   job and raises rather than reporting.
2. The run summary reported `counterexample_found` from "some tree is missing"
   **without checking the edge threshold**, so a below-threshold extremal
   example would have been written to an artifact as a counterexample.  Caught
   when run G (which sits deliberately one edge below its threshold) started
   finding 8-regular graphs.  That run was killed and re-run after the fix;
   its first attempt contributes nothing.
3. The first search objective maximised backtracking-node counts, and a later
   one used a 16-sample randomised estimator - the annealer optimised the
   estimator's noise.  Both were replaced by the exact deterministic
   homomorphism count.  Runs made with them were discarded, not merged.

Smoke-test artifacts produced by the buggy intermediate versions were deleted
rather than left in the directory, since a saved "best graph" with the wrong
edge count would be actively misleading.

### 5.5 What the numbers say

At the primary instance the best the search could do, among the 42 trees that
could actually be the missing one, is a graph in which the scarcest such tree
still has about **4 x 10^7 distinct embeddings**.  The overall scarcest tree is
the star, at 725,760 - and the star is provably *always* present at 61 edges,
because 61 edges force a vertex of degree 9.  So the search never got within
seven orders of magnitude of a counterexample.

The unconstrained search converges on near-regular graphs (degrees 8 and 9),
which is exactly the region Eaton-Tiner's theorem certifies as safe.  Forcing
the search into the region where a counterexample must live (delta = 5) makes
embeddings *more* abundant, not less.  Both facts point the same way, though
neither is a proof.

## 6. What this does not say

* It does **not** show that the Erdos-Sos conjecture holds at n = 15.  There
  are C(105,61) = 8.0 x 10^29 labelled graphs on 15 vertices with exactly 61
  edges, roughly 6 x 10^17 of them up to isomorphism.  The search visited a
  minuscule fraction of that.  "I did not find a counterexample" and "no
  counterexample exists" are different sentences and only the first is
  supported here.
* The only genuinely **exhaustive** statements here are local, and are labelled
  as such: the census over sampled graphs is exhaustive over *the sample*, and
  the 1-swap hull is exhaustive over *that neighbourhood*.  Neither says
  anything about graphs outside those sets.
* The structural narrowing in section 4 that gives delta = 5 is CONDITIONAL on
  two theorems I read only through a third party's statement of them
  (Eaton-Tiner Thm 1.4 and Yuan-Zhang Thm 1.8, both as quoted in
  arXiv:1403.5430).  I did not verify their proofs.  The unconstrained runs do
  not rely on them.
* The identification of t = 10 as the open case at n = 15 rests on reading
  "k" in the literature as tree order.  I verified that reading against two
  primary sources but could not obtain Tiner-Tomlin [TiTo22].  The alternative
  reading was searched too, and also produced nothing.
